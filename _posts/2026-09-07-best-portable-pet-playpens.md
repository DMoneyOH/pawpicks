## What to look for in a portable pet playpen

Size comes first. Measure your pet standing and lying down, then pick an enclosure that lets it turn around and rest without pressing against the mesh. A cat wants height more than floor area, since a bored cat climbs. A small dog wants floor space to pace and circle before it settles.

Weight and pack size decide whether the enclosure actually leaves the house. A pop-up model that folds into a flat case travels far more often than a heavy panel system that lives in the garage and never sees a trip.

Check the top. An open enclosure works for a dog that stays put, but a removable zipper top matters for cats and jumpy puppies, and it adds shade on a sunny lawn.

Look at the floor and the fabric. A sewn-in base keeps a digger from tunneling out and blocks damp grass from soaking through. Mesh should feel tight and stitched twice at the seams, since that is where a chewer starts to work.

Match the pen to the pet, not the marketing photo. A calm senior cat and a nippy puppy ask for different things.
